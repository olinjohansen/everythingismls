### Cold open: why everything is MLS

*01:31–06:41 · Olin, Andy* — The hosts introduce the show, David Sloan Wilson, and the idea that understanding evolution properly means understanding multilevel selection.

- Wilson, D. S. (1975). A theory of group selection. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 72(1), 143–146. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.72.1.143) · [Free](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC432258/)
  - Introduced the trait-group model, placing selection within and among groups on a single continuum: the founding paper of modern multilevel selection theory. *(03:16, raised by Andy)*
- Darwin, C. (1871). The Descent of Man, and Selection in Relation to Sex. John Murray. [Link](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34967) · (free)
  - Darwin's early argument that groups with more cooperative members prevail over other groups, an intuition multilevel selection theory later made rigorous. *(03:16, raised by Andy)*
- Reflections on Papers Past. (2017). Revisiting Wilson 1975. Reflections on Papers Past (blog). [Link](https://reflectionsonpaperspast.com/?p=255) · (free)
  - David Sloan Wilson recounts how his 1975 model began, prompted by a paper on the vertical migration of zooplankton. *(04:22, raised by Andy)*

### Tinbergen's four questions

*05:15–06:41; 11:19–22:14 · Olin, David, Andy* — Function, mechanism, development, and evolutionary history: the four complementary questions that frame the whole episode.

- Tinbergen, N. (1963). On aims and methods of ethology. Zeitschrift für Tierpsychologie, 20(4), 410–433. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1963.tb01161.x)
  - Proposed four complementary questions (survival value, causation, development, and evolutionary history) for fully explaining any behavior. *(05:15–06:41; 11:19–22:14, raised by Olin)*
- Tinbergen, N. (2005). On aims and methods of ethology. Animal Biology, 55(4), 297–321.
  - Facsimile reprint of the 1963 paper that established the four questions. *(11:19–22:14, raised by Olin)*
- Bateson, P., & Laland, K. N. (2013). Tinbergen's four questions: An appreciation and an update. Trends in Ecology & Evolution, 28(12), 712–718. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2013.09.013)
  - A fifty-year reappraisal arguing that the four questions remain essential, with updates for modern biology. *(11:19–22:14, raised by David)*
- Bergman, T. J., & Beehner, J. C. (2022). Leveling with Tinbergen: Four levels simplified to causes and consequences. Evolutionary Anthropology, 31(1), 12–19. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1002/evan.21931) · [Free](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/171823/evan21931_am.pdf?sequence=1)
  - Arranges the four questions on a timescale of causes and consequences, clarifying how they complement one another. *(11:19–22:14, raised by David)*

### Mayr: proximate vs. ultimate causation; founder effects

*18:55–27:16; 32:19; 1:02:14 · David, Andy, Olin* — Mayr's 1961 split between 'how' (proximate) and 'why' (ultimate) causes, plus his founder principle.

- Mayr, E. (1961). Cause and effect in biology: Kinds of causes, predictability, and teleology are viewed by a practicing biologist. Science, 134(3489), 1501–1506. [Link](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1707986)
  - Distinguished proximate ('how') from ultimate ('why') causes in biology, a distinction that parallels Tinbergen's four questions. *(18:55; 22:14–27:16; 32:19, raised by Andy)*
- Laland, K. N., et al. (2011). Cause and effect in biology revisited: Is Mayr's proximate-ultimate dichotomy still useful? Science, 334(6062), 1512–1516. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1210879) · [Free](https://lalandlab.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/files/2015/08/Publication174.pdf)
  - Argues that Mayr's proximate–ultimate split obscures how development and behavior can themselves shape evolution. *(18:55–27:16, raised by David)*
- Mayr, E. (1942). Systematics and the Origin of Species. Columbia University Press.
  - Introduced the founder principle: a population started by a few individuals carries only a chance sample of its parent population's variation. *(1:02:14, raised by Andy)*
- Mayr, E. (1954). Change of genetic environment and evolution. In J. Huxley, A. C. Hardy, & E. B. Ford (Eds.), Evolution as a Process (pp. 157–180). Allen and Unwin.
  - Developed founder effects into 'genetic revolutions' that can drive rapid divergence of isolated populations. *(1:02:14, raised by Andy)*

### Evolutionary mismatch

*22:14–26:38 · Andy, David* — Why ice cream tastes so good: traits built for past environments can misfire in new ones.

- Neel, J. V. (1962). Diabetes mellitus: A "thrifty" genotype rendered detrimental by "progress"? American Journal of Human Genetics, 14(4), 353–362. [Link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1932342/) · (free)
  - The 'thrifty genotype': genes that favor fat storage under scarcity become harmful amid modern abundance. *(22:14–25:42, raised by Andy)*
- Eaton, S. B., Konner, M., & Shostak, M. (1988). Stone agers in the fast lane: Chronic degenerative diseases in evolutionary perspective. American Journal of Medicine, 84(4), 739–749. [Link](https://www.melvinkonner.com/images/PDFs/articles/eatonkonnerstoneagersfastlane88.pdf) · (free)
  - Argued that chronic 'diseases of civilization' reflect a discordance between Stone Age biology and modern life. *(25:42–26:38, raised by David)*
- Bowlby, J. (1969). Attachment and Loss: Vol. 1. Attachment. Basic Books. *(citation details being verified)*
  - Introduced the 'environment of evolutionary adaptedness,' the conditions to which a trait is adapted. *(26:05, raised by David)*
- Griffiths, P. E., & Bourrat, P. (2023). Integrating evolutionary, developmental and physiological mismatch. Evolution, Medicine, and Public Health, 11(1), 277–286. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1093/emph/eoad023) · [Free](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10446139/)
  - Reviews the history of the mismatch concept and integrates its evolutionary, developmental, and physiological forms. *(25:42–26:38, raised by David)*
- Li, N. P., van Vugt, M., & Colarelli, S. M. (2018). The evolutionary mismatch hypothesis: Implications for psychological science. Current Directions in Psychological Science, 27(1), 38–44. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721417731378)
  - Applies evolutionary mismatch to psychology: adaptive minds misfiring in novel environments. *(25:42–26:38, raised by David)*
- Sih, A., Ferrari, M. C. O., & Harris, D. J. (2011). Evolution and behavioural responses to human-induced rapid environmental change. Evolutionary Applications, 4(2), 367–387. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1752-4571.2010.00166.x)
  - Reviews how rapid human-induced environmental change leaves many species' behavior mismatched to new conditions. *(26:24, raised by David)*
